## Highlights

### The pledge went out to a hundred maintainers (D1)

This week the pledge stopped waiting to be found. **#34** added the invitation
tooling. `build-recipients.py` picks unsigned maintainers from the ecosystem
map. `send.sh` opens one personal issue per maintainer, spaced 10 to 20 minutes
apart, and re-reads the signatory list before each one, so nobody who signed
mid-run is asked. **118 invitations** went out during the week. They account for
most of the week's 32 comments, which is why the counter is so high.

**Thirteen new signatures** merged, in five PRs (#158 and #166 with five each,
plus #95, #160 and #167). Several signatories came in through the invitation
itself: their issue closed when their signature landed. The list now has **35
signatories**. The new ones include developers and founders behind
**Cardanoscan, Masumi and NMKR, Charli3, Andamio, ADA Handle (Kora Labs),
No.Witness Labs, NUFI/AdaLite and TxBody**, and Hydra at Input Output, along
with independent contributors.

The replies were as useful as the signatures:

- **Some maintainers support the principles but won't sign.** One will not put
  their name to a document that can still change. Another prefers not to sign
  anything but will "do my best to adhere to its principles", and points out
  that opinionated design can make parallel utilities legitimate. That second
  point belongs in the pledge's next revision.
- **One reply questioned IO's standing to ask**, citing past ecosystem funding
  decisions. That is a credibility gap the initiative closes by how it acts, not
  by what the pledge says.
- **One maintainer has left the Cardano ecosystem.** #164 removed them from the
  map's team listings so the recipient builder can't pick them again. A

### `cardano-init`: an Evolution template a backend can use (D3, #87, #79)

**#87** (@0xBora) makes the **Evolution gift-card template return unsigned
transactions**. Before, `createGiftCard` and `redeemGiftCard` signed and
submitted internally, which only works when the builder is also the signer. Now
build and sign are separate steps, matching the MeshJS template: build returns
the unsigned transaction, a new `signAndSubmit` signs it, and `node.ts` exposes
the provider client for a backend that builds without a wallet. The motivation
is downstream: the Developer Portal is building a sample dApp on top of a
`cardano-init` project, with a backend that builds and a browser wallet that
signs. The portal's dApp templates will start from `cardano-init` instead of
their own scaffolding.

**#79**, open since 20 August, fixed the **missing contributor attribution in
release notes**. git-cliff silently skips GitHub handle resolution when it has
no token or can't infer the repository, and on a fresh release branch it had
neither. `cliff.toml` now pins the remote, `RELEASING.md` passes
`GITHUB_TOKEN` and documents the pitfall, and the changelog was regenerated
with `by @handle` restored on every release.

### The smart wallet got a spec, pluggable restrictions and two off-chain drivers (D5)

Fifteen commits from Francisco Joray (**+4,740 / −196 across 43 files**) took the
smart wallet from a validator to a full exploration.

The **263-line `spec.md`** sets out the model. A wallet is one NFT-bearing UTxO
behind a fixed **M-of-N signature floor**, with the members and threshold read
from a settings UTxO. Everything beyond the floor is a **delegated withdraw-0
script** listed in the datum as `Pairs<ScriptHash, Data>`, and the `Data` is
that script's own mutable state. Spending requires the floor *and* every
delegated script, while the spending validator knows nothing about what those
scripts check. That is what keeps the restrictions open-ended.

The on-chain work filled in the rules that keep this safe:

- **A script can't join without validating itself.** A script added at mint or
  by `UpdateConfig` must be registered in the same transaction, and its
  `publish` handler checks its own starting state.
- **A script can't be unregistered behind the wallet's back.** Unregistration is
  accepted only when the script is actually leaving the wallet, and `Close`
  unregisters all of them. Otherwise anyone could unregister a delegated script
  and freeze the wallet.
- **A `Deposit` redeemer** lets a depositor add funds without being able to
  spend them or change the config.

Two **example restrictions** ship with it, one of each kind. `spending_limit` is
stateless, with its bound fixed at compile time. `spending_window` is stateful:
it records the last spend and limits how often the wallet can pay out, reading
`now` from the validity range's lower bound. The off-chain side arrived in one
day: a **MeshJS driver** with e2e suites for the wallet and for the withdrawal
scripts, and a **Tx3** protocol (792-line `main.tx3`) with its own devnet suite.

The spec is upfront that this is a first version. The admin is still a
compile-time parameter, and withdrawal scripts are per-wallet. Both are slated
to become ordinary, shareable delegated scripts.

### Advanced lecture 1 grew two multi-step exercises (D4, #2011)

Paulo Bressan reorganized *Detecting vulnerabilities* into **single-step** and
**multi-step** attacks (**+1,687 / −129**). The gift-card shop stays as the
single-step exercise, and two new projects cover the multi-step side. Each one
ships an open validator and a fixed one:

- **The splitter (resource limit).** Anyone may donate to a shared pot, and any
  token counts as a donation. An attacker donates worthless dust tokens a few at
  a time until reading the pot costs more execution units than a transaction is
  allowed. At that point nobody can split it, and the ADA is stuck. Every step
  was allowed, and no single one broke a rule.
- **The vault's stake part.** Anyone may donate to an owner's vault, and the
  donor gets to choose the stake part of the returned UTxO, and with it who
  collects the vault's staking rewards.

Separately, every onboarding Aiken example moved to **compiler v1.1.24 and
stdlib v4**, with the tools lecture updated to match. #2011 is still open, and
review has requested changes.
