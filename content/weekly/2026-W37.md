### The last mile before the merge was security work (D5)

The DAO did not coast into `main`. Monday and Tuesday were spent closing
attack surface:

- **Tagged outputs against double satisfaction.** Refund checks moved into
  `validate_vote_tally` and now use tagged outputs, so one refund cannot be
  claimed by two tally transactions.
- **Tally DoS heuristics.** Explicit heuristics on vote tallying, so a proposal
  cannot be made uncountable by flooding it — and a design note capping the
  locks list to keep the stake datum from bloating past what a transaction can
  carry.
- **Settings read only when needed.** The validators stopped fetching the
  settings UTxO on paths that do not consult it — cheaper, and a smaller
  reference-input surface.
- **Checks moved to where they belong.** The cosign threshold check moved from
  the stake validator to the proposal validator; the redundant `create`
  threshold check and an unnecessary `is_draft` check came out.

Plus the ordinary tidying that makes a merge reviewable:
`StakePositionTokenRedeemer` → `StakeTokenRedeemer` across the tree, a
consistent import order, a dropped max-tally test that cost minutes for no
extra coverage, and both off-chain drivers re-aligned with the new on-chain
shape.

### Intermediate lectures 10–13 went up for review (D4, #2003)

Paulo Bressan opened the PR that closes the Intermediate track: **77 files,
+6,151 / −92**, four design lectures each with a runnable Aiken project and,
where the lecture has an app, a Mesh app.

- **10, Handling time** — a vesting deadline enforced through the transaction's
  validity window, with the contract never reading a clock.
- **11, Multi validators** — a gift card as one script with a `mint` and a
  `spend` handler, and a one-shot policy seeded from a UTxO.
- **12, Modifying state** — an oracle whose rate lives on the chain and changes:
  `Update` puts the UTxO back, `Delete` burns the beacon.
- **13, Reference inputs & reference scripts** — a consumer that reads the
  oracle's datum as a reference input, then the same consumer published once and
  unlocked through it.

The 19 commits inside the window are the shape of the thing being found rather
than written: the oracle's beacon **split out into its own one-shot policy** and
the two oracle lectures reworked around that split; lectures 10 and 11 given
their own projects and transaction diagrams; a Mesh app added for the gift card;
the consumer's fee paid from the wallet so the published script stays unspent;
the vesting validator switched to the **stdlib deadline check**; and a small
`validatorHash.js` helper so every script hash quoted in the prose is **read
from the example blueprints** rather than pasted.
